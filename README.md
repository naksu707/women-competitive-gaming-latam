## 🌙 The story

Women make up roughly half of the people who play video games in Latin America, yet in competitive online spaces many of them mute their mic, change their username or play only with friends.

This project follows that contrast through a scroll-based narrative: from who plays, to what happens in competitive matches, to the networks women build to stay in the game.

| Chapter | What it explores |
|---|---|
| 🌿 **Who Plays** | Women's participation in gaming across LATAM |
| ⚔️ **The Match** | Harassment and discrimination in competitive online games |
| 🎭 **Adapting** | Strategies women use to cope: hiding their voice, name or gender |
| 🤝 **Community** | Support networks and women-led communities in the region |

## Data sources

All figures in the story come from public reports and peer-reviewed research:

| Source | What it provides |
|---|---|
| [Mujeres en VG — *Mujeres y Videojuegos 2026*](https://www.mujeresenvg.cl) | Survey of ~3,000 women in 12 LATAM countries on harassment and discrimination |
| [Pesquisa Game Brasil (PGB)](https://www.pesquisagamebrasil.com.br) | Annual gamer profile in Brazil, broken down by gender |
| [ESA — *Power of Play 2025 Global Report*](https://www.theesa.com) | Global gender split of players across 21 countries |
| [Treviño & Cobos (2025), *Austral Comunicación*](https://doi.org/10.26422/aucom.2025.1403.tre) | Survey of Latin American women gamers: initiation and daily relevance |
| [UNESCO (2024) — *The gender equality quest in video games*](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000391949) | Global context on gender and gaming |
| [ADL — *Free to Play?*](https://www.adl.org/free-to-play) | Methodological reference on measuring harassment in online games |
